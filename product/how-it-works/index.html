How it works

From a bank alert to one clear answer.

Every Indian bank already sends an alert for every transaction. Spends reads that feed on your phone and measures it against your pay cycle. Here is what happens, step by step.

All of this runs on your phone. Reading, matching, scoring and forecasting happen on the device. Your transactions, SMS and notifications are never sent to a server. Security and privacy

The pipeline

Seven steps, in order, each time Spends syncs.

  1. An alert arrives

    Your bank sends an SMS for every debit and credit. Your UPI app may send a notification too. Spends reads them on the phone: bank SMS, notifications from an approved list of bank and payment apps (if you turn that on), alerts you share into Spends, and spends you add yourself.

  2. The filter drops what isn't a transaction

    OTPs, promotions, KYC messages, links and coupons are rejected before anything is read from them. Failed and declined payments are dropped too, so they never count. Reminders of a future EMI or SIP are kept apart, because they are not spends yet.

  3. The details are read

    From each real transaction Spends reads the amount, the merchant, the account or card (last 4 digits), the date and, if the alert has one, the available balance. Only those details and a short snippet of up to 80 characters are kept, never the full message.

  4. Duplicates are merged

    The same payment often arrives as a bank SMS and a UPI notification. Spends matches them and keeps one, with the clearer merchant name. A spend seen only in a notification shows as pending until it is confirmed.

  5. Refunds are linked to their purchase

    A refund is matched to the purchase it came from, within 30 days. A partial refund lowers that spend; a full refund takes it out of your totals. A refund with no matching purchase never lowers your spend.

  6. Money that isn't spending is not counted twice

    The same amount leaving one of your accounts and arriving in another within 2 days is a transfer. A bank debit that pays your credit-card bill is a bill payment, since the card spends were already counted. Your own "count this" or "don't count this" always wins.

  7. Pace, Spend health and the forecast

    From the cleaned list, Spends works out your pace against payday, your Spend health, the one action worth taking and, on Pro, the payday cash forecast. Every number can be traced back to the alerts behind it.

When does it sync? Today, new bank SMS are read when you open Spends or come back to it. Instant capture, as each SMS arrives, is coming.

Pace, in plain words

Your cycle runs from payday to the day before the next payday, not the calendar month. Pace compares your flexible spending so far (food, rides, shopping and the like) with what you usually spend by this day of the cycle.

Spend health, simply

Spend health is a score out of 100. It starts at 100 and loses points for five things, each measured against your own usual, not anyone else's:

  • pace: spending faster than usual for this point in the cycle;
  • month-end risk: the chance your money runs short before payday;
  • category drift: one category climbing well above its usual;
  • mix shift: your spending moving between categories;
  • small-spend share: many small payments adding up.

The app shows how many points each one took, so you can see why the score isn't higher.

75 and aboveOn track
50 to 74Above usual pace
Below 50A tight month
The exact weights

score = 100 − (0.28 × pace + 0.28 × month-end risk + 0.14 × category drift + 0.10 × mix shift + 0.20 × small-spend share)

Kept between 0 and 100. Deductions are rounded so they add up exactly to the gap from 100.

The payday cash forecast, and its honesty rules

The forecast (Pro) answers “where will my cash land before payday?”. It is a set of plain rules, worked out on the phone:

lowest point = cash now − bills due before payday − daily pace × days left

For “cash now” it uses the balance in your latest bank alerts, or a balance you type (a typed one wins while it is fresh). Cards and wallets are left out. “Daily pace” is the higher of your usual and your current daily spend, so it doesn't flatter you.

Bands, never a probability

The result is shown as one of three bands against the safety floor you set (the least you want in the bank): Safe, Watch (within about a week of flexible spending of your floor) or At risk (below it). Spends doesn't show a percentage chance, because it can't honestly know one.

“Not enough data” instead of a guess

The forecast refuses to answer, and tells you what to add, when:

  • the latest balance is more than 72 hours old;
  • capture hasn't synced in the last 24 hours;
  • there is less than a week of spending this cycle to measure pace from;
  • no safety floor is set.

Every forecast is stored on the phone with the inputs it used, so its accuracy can be checked later against what actually happened.

Nothing leaves the phone.

There is no bank login and no statement upload. Spends works from the alerts your bank already sends you, and keeps the results encrypted on your phone. The only server Spends has holds an optional sign-in and your plan, never your spending.
