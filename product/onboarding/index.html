Getting started

From install to your first Pulse.

What each step does, why it is there, and what Spends reads and never reads.

What Spends reads

  • Bank and UPI SMS: debits, credits, salary and balance alerts.
  • If you turn it on, notifications from an approved list of bank and payment apps.
  • Alerts you share into Spends, and spends you add yourself.

What it never reads

  • OTPs. They are skipped before anything is read from them.
  • Personal chats and other people's messages.
  • Your contacts.
  • Promotions and other non-money messages, which are skipped.

  1. Install Spends

    Spends is an Android app, in beta. Join the beta and follow the install steps on the beta site. It is Android only, because iPhone doesn't let apps read SMS.

    How to install the beta

  2. The intro

    The first screen shows what Spends is for, using an example cycle (labelled as an example): are you on track until payday, can this cycle take a purchase, and a short check each week.

    Nothing is read yet, and no permission is asked. Tap Get started.

  3. Choose how Spends sees your spends

    Pick one. You can change it later in Settings.

    • Read bank SMS on this phone (fastest, recommended). Reads bank and UPI alerts only. Everything stays on the phone. It never reads OTPs, personal chats or contacts.
    • Add spends myself. Spends reads nothing. You add spends by hand, about a minute a day, or share a bank alert into Spends.
    • Explore with example data. Fictional data, clearly labelled. Switch to your own data in one tap.

  4. The SMS permission

    If you chose to read bank SMS, Spends first explains what it will do, in its own words, before Android asks:

    • It reads financial SMS only: debits, credits and UPI alerts.
    • Everything is processed on your phone. Nothing is uploaded.
    • OTPs and personal messages are ignored.
    • Only the details it needs are kept (amount, merchant, account, date and a short snippet), not the full SMS.
    • You can say no and still add spends by hand.

    Tap I understand, continue, then allow SMS in Android's own dialog. You can turn it off later in Android settings.

  5. Scan

    Spends reads the past bank and UPI alerts already in your inbox, on the phone. Messages that aren't about money, such as OTPs, promotions and personal chats, are skipped before anything is read from them. This is how Spends can show you your history on day one, with no bank login and no statement upload.

  6. Reveal: where your last salaries went

    When the scan finishes, Spends shows what it found: how many salary credits, merchants and subscriptions, which day your salary usually lands and roughly how much, and the places you return to most. If it isn't sure about your salary, it says “Estimated, please confirm”.

  7. Confirm your baseline

    Two things are required: your payday (day of the month) and your monthly income. If Spends found them in your SMS, they are filled in for you to check.

    The rest is optional: your usual food, transport and shopping spend, rent and fixed bills, and a savings target. These make pace and alerts more accurate. Tap Get started.

  8. Your first Pulse

    Pulse shows your Spend health out of 100, the cycle rail from payday to payday, how many days to payday, and how far ahead of (or within) your usual pace you are. Under it, one suggested action if there is one.

    The payday cash forecast needs a safety floor and a recent balance. Until it has them, it says “Not enough data” and asks for what is missing.

  9. Your first week

    • First insight. Within the first days, Spends looks for one thing you may not have known: a category drifting up, a forgotten subscription, a run of small spends.
    • Correct Activity. Open Activity and fix any merchant or category that looks wrong. Spends keeps your corrections. Tap + to add cash spends.
    • Weekly check. Once a week, a one-minute review of the last 7 days against a usual week. Mark whether it was useful.
    • Link your cards. In Wallet, drop each card on the account that pays it.

  10. Optional extras

    • Notification access. Lets Spends pick up alerts from your bank and UPI apps too. It only accepts an approved list of banking and payment apps. Turn it on from Spends, and off in Android Settings → Notification access.
    • App lock. Settings → App lock opens Spends with your fingerprint or face.
    • Pro trial. Once Pro opens, you can try it free for 30 days, once per Google account. Right now Pro is a waitlist, and the beta already has everything unlocked. About the plans.
